# Summary
These Interactive Data Science and Visualization open educational resources offer hands-on instructional materials for crafting compelling digital experiences which enable users to interactively engage with computation and data. Originally taught at the University of California Berkeley in 2025 (Catalogue) and in a number of guest lectures and workshops across multiple schools and programs (PottingerTeaching), these labs and lectures provide a multi-disciplinary approach. Specifically, these open source materials begin with a traditional visualization perspective starting in information design (Munzner) and perception science (Ware) but then extend to meet today’s needs by considering a broad array of perspectives such as media studies (Hall), human-computer interaction (Harrison), user research (Shneiderman), and game design (Schell). Altogether, through applied skills labs and guided practical projects which explore ideas of software engineering to support this interactivity, this course offers scientists, engineers, journalists, and others an opportunity to critically consider the role of the designer / developer in constructing interfaces to information and to understand the unique possibility of digital media to invite audiences to co-create meaning (VictorDead). Historically situated (Plato) but embracing modern tools such as in the employment of AI, these re-usable resources invite instructors and learners to foster rooted practices in the creation of compelling interactive “media for thinking the unthinkable” (VictorMedia) necessary to support modern scientific exploration through computation.

# Statement of Need
Existing open data visualization instruction often takes a perception science and user-centered design approach (MunznerYoutube) but may lean on using charting systems or static drawing (Cario). Therefore, some valued resources choose not to center what new bespoke experiences are possible with custom programming (MarcusWu). In response to the current offering as well as years of lecturing within existing curricula (PottingerTeaching), the presented open source educational materials instead assume some programming capability which enable the creation of new custom interactive graphical forms, taking the same commonly-taught classical principles (ClevelandMcGill) but freeing them to be used as ingredients and not as prescriptive recipes (PottingerTools). In this reconceptualization of information design rooted in the historic practice of exploring new visual forms (Tufte), we reach beyond the standard list of charts towards giving students the capacity to build pieces similar to the custom interactive experiences which often light up the classroom but which also often remain out of reach for students to actually construct (Harris; Rees). This embrace of software engineering to augment information design enables teachers and learners to update traditional data visualization instruction to support applications in science-informed policymaking (Enroads; PottingerPlasticsTool; PottingerPlasticsPaper) and interactive science (VictorExplorable), meeting today’s opportunities created by computers ever growing capacity to react to the user (VictorDead). In total, these resources seek to empower data professionals to blend evidence-based design with the expressive capabilities of development.

# Learning Modules
Through hands on-projects, the **objective** of these open source educational materials is to both empower students with heightened capacity to critically interrogate and design digital media as well as the capability to build rich bespoke interactive experiences. Towards that end, in addition to discussing ethics and accessibility, these materials are provided through a multi-part **design** which offers a different “perspectives” on data visualization:

 - Visualization as representation explores a traditional perception science approach to information design (Ware).
 - Visualization as task brings students into the modern human-centered design conception of data visualization (Munzner).
 - Visualization as message branches into a sociology, anthropology, and media studies (DiagrammaticCovid; Hall).
 - Visualization as dialogue unlocks the unique capabilities of digital interactivity that explore audiences as co-creators of meaning (VictorDead), including through game design (Portnow).

This mix of traditional lecture **content** with hands-on labs and projects manifests as HTML-rendered course materials^[Each entry includes its own citations which may extend beyond those listed here.]. However, most lessons also include captioned video and PDF materials as supplement. Altogether, these offer the **experience** of programming to not just learn interactive information design but to feel and understand the role of human iteration. Finally, the course concludes with a critical but applied consideration of using AI in the construction of visualizations.

# Background and Use
These materials were taught as a full course at the University of California, Berkeley in 2025 as Statistics 198: Interactive Data Science and Visualization (Catalogue), offered for both graduate and undergraduate credit^[The original offering was for two credit hours for upper-division undergraduate or graduate instruction though use of some optional materials may allow for use in a 3 credit hour setting.]. Additionally, these materials have also served different programs and universities as components in other instruction or as workshops (PottingerTeaching). Individual lessons may provide topic-specific supplement to other instruction, modules may offer a treatment of a topical area for learning, and the class as a whole may allow for an approximate semester of hybrid hands-on and lecture-based exploration.

# Software
Due to the specific needs of a blended design and engineering instruction, these materials are compiled from markdown and YAML to HTML via custom Python as described in the repository. All of these materials are made available under a Creative Commons license with code elements released as open source. Using modern technologies like Web Assembly, these open source resources offer pathways to complete all instruction without installing local software beyond a modern web browser.
